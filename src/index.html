RFP & Pricing Process Analyzer

An internal tool for analysing an OEM's aftermarket pricing process and RFP. Work through the 9-step pricing canvas, mark which challenges the OEM's RFP or process points to, and generate a pain heat map and a prioritised discussion roadmap, all for your own preparation and not for the OEM to see.

1
Map the RFP to the canvas

Process Matrix

Walk through the 9 steps of the pricing process. For each step the OEM's RFP touches, open the challenge library and mark the challenges that match what the RFP is actually asking for, across Foundational, Integrated, and Advanced tiers.

Output: a complete set of marked challenges by process step and capability tier, reflecting what this OEM's RFP is pointing at.
2
See where it concentrates

Pain Heat Map

Instantly generated from your selections. Shows where the OEM's pricing requirements concentrate, by process step (where in the flow) and by tier (how advanced the capability gap is). Darker cells mean more requirements marked.

Output: a visual of where this OEM's RFP concentrates, to help you prioritise your response.
3
Sequence your response

Priority Roadmap

All marked challenges sequenced into a Diagnose → Transform → Execute model. Ordered so foundational data and steering capabilities land before operational execution. Includes technology recommendations to reference in your response.

Output: a prioritised internal roadmap, exportable as a PDF for your own reference or RFP-response prep.

How to use this tool

This is an internal tool for analysing an OEM's aftermarket pricing process or incoming RFP. It is never shown to the OEM. It maps the pricing process across 9 steps, 5 canvas phases and 3 capability tiers, and helps you mark which challenges the OEM's RFP or process points to, then turns that into a heat map and a prioritised roadmap for your own use.

Getting started
  1. Type the OEM or account name on the welcome screen so your work is labelled.
  2. On the Process Matrix, click any cell to read the best-practice workflow, personas and reference use cases for that step.
  3. If the OEM's RFP or process touches this step, scroll down inside the panel and tick the challenges that match what the RFP is asking for, directly there, with no separate step.
  4. Open Pain Heat Map to see where the OEM's requirements concentrate, and Priority Roadmap for a Diagnose → Transform → Execute sequence plus technical recommendations.
What are the reference use cases?

The codes like U01, U02 shown inside each step are internal process reference codes. Each one describes a concrete product capability: the actors involved, what triggers it, and what it produces. Use them to judge how well the product covers what the OEM is asking for at that step.

Saving & sharing your work
  • Auto-save: as you work, your selections are kept in this browser on this device. Reopening the file restores them.
  • Save session: use Save session on the Process Matrix tab, or Export .json in the Session menu, to download a small .json file. This is the reliable way to keep a copy, move it to another machine, or send it to a colleague.
  • Load session: use Load session to open a previously saved .json file.
  • Export PDF report: produces a printable summary (heat map, marked challenges, roadmap and recommendations) for internal use or RFP-response preparation. Enable Background graphics in the print dialog, or the shading prints white.
  • New engagement: clears everything and the saved session, for a clean start with a new OEM.
Where your data goes

Everything runs locally in your browser. Nothing you type or tick is sent to any server: the file has no backend and no tracking. Auto-save uses your browser's local storage; if that is unavailable (for example in private-browsing mode or when opened from some email attachments), the tool still works but will not auto-restore. Use Save session to keep your work.

Tips
  • The distributed build is a single self-contained file. You can email it internally, put it on a shared drive, or open it by double-clicking. No install and no server needed.
  • Best viewed in a current version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.
  • For a clean PDF, use the browser print dialog's “Save as PDF” with default A4 margins.
